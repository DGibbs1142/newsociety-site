Issue 06 Sunday, November 1, 2026

a first World Series, a first Game 7, and country’s first clean sweep

Three things happened this week that had never happened before. That is more firsts than most months manage.

Sports

Milwaukee won the World Series for the first time in the franchise’s existence.

Four games to one over Tampa Bay, clinched on October 28, and it is the first title the Brewers have ever won — not a drought ended, the first one. William Contreras took series MVP. It is also the first baseball championship the city has had since 1957. They got there by sweeping the Dodgers, the team that had swept them out of the same round a year earlier, which had never been done before either.

Sports

The WNBA needed a seventh game to find a champion, and a team in its second season won it.

Golden State beat Atlanta 68-63 on October 31 to win the first Game 7 in WNBA Finals history. The Finals only went best-of-seven last year, and that series ended in four, so this was a game nobody had ever played. The Valkyries trailed by six at the half and won the second half 44-33, and Finals MVP Gabby Williams is most of the reason: 2 points before the break, 28 after it, including three three-pointers inside one minute of the third quarter. Kiah Stokes took 13 rebounds, and Atlanta pulled within three inside the final eleven seconds before Veronica Burton closed it out from the line. Golden State played its first game in May 2025 and reached the playoffs that same year, which no expansion team had done. Atlanta joined the league in 2008 and has now lost four Finals without winning one.

Music

Country holds the entire top five of the Hot 100. That has never happened.

Billboard confirmed all five of the chart’s top positions belong to country songs for the first time in the Hot 100’s history. Whatever you make of the genre, a clean sweep of the top five is not a fluke of one big single. It is the kind of result that only shows up when something underneath has already moved.

Pop culture

“You Can See Everything” is winning the weekend.

It took the top Friday spot with $3.56 million, holding off Clayface. Two weeks ago the same film was sitting at 99 percent on the audience score, which is a number you almost never see on anything. The crowd worked it out well before the box office did.

Anime

January is shaping up as a sequel season.

Sakamoto Days, MASHLE, Akane-banashi and Shangri-La Frontier are all lined up to return. The fall slate that arrived all at once in early October has barely settled and the next wave is already scheduled. Meanwhile One Piece turned up inside Fortnite and JISOO from BLACKPINK cosplayed Lucy from Cyberpunk Edgerunners. Anime stopped staying in its lane a long time ago.

Fashion

Two opposite theories about what a shop is for.

New Balance put out a Made in America 990v7 in muted grey — the least attention-seeking release strategy in footwear, and one that has worked for them for years. At the other end, a French luxury house handed part of a store over to an exhibition of the Surrealist painter Roland Topor. One sells by saying almost nothing. The other sells by turning the room into a gallery.

Issue 05 Sunday, October 18, 2026

the Brewers waited 44 years, Kyrie waited 586 days, Tinashe waited a decade

A theme turned up on its own this week. Almost everything worth writing about took a long time to get here.

Sports

The Brewers swept the team that swept them. Nobody had ever done that before.

Los Angeles swept Milwaukee out of the NLCS last year. This year Milwaukee returned it exactly — four games to none — and it is the first time in postseason history that a team has swept the opponent that swept it the season before. It sends the Brewers to the World Series for the first time since 1982. Forty-four years. In the same stretch Kyrie Irving played again after 586 days out with a torn ACL, led Dallas in scoring in his first game back, and did it on a floor that also had Cooper Flagg standing on it.

Music

Tinashe made the top 10 for the first time. It only took her more than a decade.

“Popstar” debuted at No. 6 on the Billboard 200 — her highest-charting album ever and her first top 10, after years of being the artist other artists quietly cite. The same week, Taylor Swift took “Patient Zero” to No. 1 on the Hot 100 and kept “The Life of a Showgirl” on top of the album chart. Both of those are the music business working exactly as advertised. Only one of them is a surprise.

Fashion

Kris Brock came back to Paris five years after walking away.

He showed BROCK, a new label under his own name, half a decade after stepping back from the brand that made him. Fashion does not usually hold a door open that long. Jimmy Choo turned thirty the same month and marked it with Pearls of Wisdom, a capsule from creative director Sandra Choi built around the house motif.

Anime

Chainsaw Man turned four, and the Assassins Arc is still in production.

Four years since it first aired and the next arc has not landed. Being a fan of this one is mostly an exercise in patience, and somehow nobody has left. The fall slate that arrived all at once in early October has settled in by now, and Dragon Ball Super: Beerus revealed its ending theme, “Kan Peki No Peki” by VK Blanka.

Pop culture

New York Comic Con turned twenty, and a book people have carried since 2018 finally has a cast.

NYCC closed its twentieth year at the Javits Center. The panel worth watching was Children of Blood and Bone — Amandla Stenberg on joining the cast, Chiwetel Ejiofor breaking down King Saran, Thuso Mbedu describing how she learned to fight with the staff. Twenty years ago this convention was a room full of people apologizing for what they were into. Now it is the room everybody is trying to get into.

Issue 04 Sunday, October 11, 2026

Anne Hathaway disappears on purpose, and the NBA redraws its map

Five movies in one year will do that to a person. Plus a league that rearranged itself over the summer, a fall anime slate that landed all at once, and a band fifty years in that cannot stop adding dates.

Pop culture

Anne Hathaway made five movies this year, then vanished into a puff of smoke.

On the day Verity opened she posted a video talking about heading to the premiere, then disappeared mid-sentence in a cartoon cloud while a member of her team wandered in asking where she went. It was the punchline to a joke she had been building all press tour. She is 43, pregnant with her third, and says her schedule is her kids’ schedule now. Verity went to number one anyway.

Sports

The NBA reshuffled itself over the summer. Baseball spent the week throwing out the bracket.

LeBron James left the Lakers for Philadelphia on a two-year, eight-million-dollar deal — about four million a season for a man who had been making fifty. Minnesota sent Naz Reid and a stack of first-round picks to Charlotte for LaMelo Ball, finally putting a real point guard next to Anthony Edwards. Neither bet answers for anything until the season opens October 21. Baseball, meanwhile, spent the week discarding favorites: Tampa Bay swept the Yankees in three, and Cleveland lost the first two at home to the White Sox before winning three straight to take the series. The Championship round starts now — Dodgers at Brewers tonight, Guardians at Rays tomorrow.

Anime

The fall season did not trickle in. It landed all at once.

Mushoku Tensei closed out season three, and inside seventy-two hours The Apothecary Diaries season three and Tokyo Revengers: War of the Three Titans arrived on October 2, Black Clover season two on the 3rd, and Blue Box season two on the 4th. Dandivine followed on the 7th, A Certain Dark Item on the 9th. Four returns in three days, then two more before the week was out.

Music

Rush said they were finished. They are fifty-eight shows into saying otherwise.

The Fifty Something tour has Geddy Lee and Alex Lifeson playing together for the first time since Neil Peart died, with German drummer Anika Nilles behind the kit. It opened in Los Angeles in June and now runs through Vancouver in December across twenty-four cities, expanded again and again because people kept buying the tickets.

Fashion

Teyana Taylor is getting her own Jordan.

The Teyana Taylor x Air Jordan 16 is expected in summer 2027, which is a long time to make people wait for something they already want. She has spent almost twenty years doing about five jobs at once — singing, dancing, directing, acting, styling — and a signature shoe reads less like a launch than like the industry finally catching up.

Issue 03 Sunday, October 4, 2026

a 121-loss team is still playing, and fall anime landed all at once

Two weeks folded into one. The team nobody took seriously is still alive, your queue refilled in three days, and a Space Age house in Paris got a new voice.

Sports

Two years ago the White Sox lost 121 games. They are two wins from the ALCS.

They won 6-3, then 7-3, both in Houston, and took the Wild Card Series without needing a third game — the same franchise that set the modern loss record in 2024. Then they went into Cleveland on Saturday and shut the Guardians out 3-0 to open the Division Series. The Yankees ended Boston 9-0 and 9-2, then lost their own opener 1-0 in Tampa.

Anime

Mushoku Tensei ended, and the fall season replaced it inside a week.

Season three closed with episode 14, “Resolve.” Then everything arrived at once: The Apothecary Diaries season three and Tokyo Revengers: War of the Three Titans on October 2, Black Clover season two on the 3rd, Blue Box season two on the 4th. Four returns in three days. The gap between seasons barely exists anymore.

Music

Madonna won seven VMAs. Taylor Swift won something that did not exist before.

Madonna was the night’s biggest winner with seven, including artist of the year and best collaboration for “Bring Your Love” with Sabrina Carpenter. Swift accepted MTV’s first-ever Artist Director Honors, then premiered the “Patient Zero” video she directed herself — Colin Farrell and Dakota Johnson star, Emmanuel Lubezki shot it.

Fashion

Courrèges handed its codes to a designer from the Phoebe Philo school.

Drew Henry showed his first collection for the house at the Palais de Tokyo on Wednesday — ribbed knits, vinyl, the A-line dress, all pulled toward something looser and louder than the brand usually allows. Henry trained at Central Saint Martins and worked under Phoebe Philo at Celine and at her own label. Paris wraps this week.

Pop culture

Fincher is making a Cliff Booth movie, and the trailer is here.

The first look at “The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth” arrived in late September, in IMAX from November 25. Brad Bird’s “RAY GUNN” got its first trailer the same morning. Two directors swinging at genre on the same day.

Issue 01 Sunday, September 20, 2026

Ole Miss stuns LSU, Ella Langley owns the chart

Week one of NewSociety. No filler, just what mattered.

Sports

Ole Miss 24, LSU 19. Oxford made Kiffin hear every second.

Trinidad Chambliss shredded his old coach’s new team in front of a record 70,033 at Vaught-Hemingway. LSU’s late push died on a kick that sailed wide right. Oh, and the Bears hung 59 on the Panthers in Week 1. Loudest week of the season so far.

Music

Ella Langley is racing herself up the chart.

Two of the top three songs in the country are hers. Drake is stuck at number two, wedged between them. And STELLA LEFTY’s "Boston" keeps climbing, the story nobody’s telling yet.

Pop culture

Spider-Man just dethroned Star Wars.

"Brand New Day" is now the highest-grossing movie ever at the domestic box office, passing "The Force Awakens." It’s also the only franchise with two films in America’s all-time top five.

Fashion

Fashion Month is on.

New York, London, Milan, Paris, back to back into early October. Only have time for one city? Pick the one whose street style you’d actually wear.

Anime

Fall anime starts October 2.

The Apothecary Diaries is back for season three. Black Clover and Tokyo Revengers land the next day, and the Magic Knight Rayearth reboot arrives October 7. Clear your queue.
